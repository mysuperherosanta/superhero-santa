# What you need to do to get the site live

Everything here is free except the domain. Set each account up **in your own name and email**, since it's your book and brand. Nate gets added as a helper, so he can do the technical work without owning anything.

## This week (to get the site live)

1. **Domain.** Make sure `mysuperherosanta.com` is registered to you (your name, your email, auto-renew on). Tell Nate which company it's with (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace, Cloudflare…).
2. **GitHub account.** Sign up free at github.com. Send Nate your username.
3. **Create the site's home on GitHub.** Click **New repository** → name it `superherosantaclaus` → choose **Public** → leave everything else unticked → **Create**.
4. **Invite Nate.** In that repository: **Settings → Collaborators → Add people** → Nate's username (`n8patterson`). Nate then uploads the site and switches it on.
5. **Domain settings.** Either log in to your domain company with Nate on a quick call, or add him as a delegate/team member there. He'll add four records that point the domain at the site. It usually goes live within an hour.

## Next (before you start promoting)

6. **Email list.** Sign up for **MailerLite** (free up to ~1,000 subscribers, includes an automated welcome series) or **Kit** (free up to 10,000 subscribers). Then add Nate as a team member. This replaces the "email us" buttons with a real signup form, so you collect a list for launch day.
7. **Google Search Console.** Sign in at search.google.com/search-console with your Google account and add `mysuperherosanta.com`, then add Nate as a user. This shows which Google searches find the site.
8. **Cloudflare (free).** Sign up at cloudflare.com. It gives free visitor stats now, and it's where a small leads/SEO dashboard would live later if you want one.
9. **Tell Nate the small fixes you want** on the homepage picture, so they get done in one batch.

## Decisions only you can make

- **The name "Superhero Santa."** The publishing plan found a pending trademark application for "SUPERHERO SANTA" (filed May 2026, covering animated entertainment), and the USPTO often treats that as related to children's books. Before printing covers, consider a ~$300–500 clearance opinion from a trademark attorney, or plan to brand the series under a more distinctive name. Ask Nate for that section of the plan.
- **Which email service** (MailerLite vs Kit). Either is fine; MailerLite is the easier start.
